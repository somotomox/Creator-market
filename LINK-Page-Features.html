01

How to read this

Feature ID

Page ID then a sequence, so C1.04 is the fourth feature on the creator profile. Stable references for tickets, test cases and change requests.

Origin

New is work that does not exist in the prototype. Harden is something that exists and needs fixing, finishing or re-fitting. The split matters because the entire commercial case rests on the second number being large.

Size

S roughly 1 to 3 hours. M roughly 4 to 8. L roughly 9 to 16. Indicative planning bands, not a quote. They exist to show where effort concentrates, and they are deliberately coarse.

A caution on the origin split

The Harden classification assumes the prototype contains what the earlier audit recorded, and that it is reachable and workable. Prototype access was still unconfirmed when this was written. If the build is not available, every hardening item becomes new work, and this inventory roughly doubles. That is the single assumption on which the whole estimate rests.

Five of these are large and easy to under-estimate

X.06 time zones, X.07 money states, X.08 the cancellation engine, X.12 reminder scheduling and X.16 webhook handling have no screen of their own, so they are invisible on a page-by-page plan. Between them they are the product's actual machinery, and all five are new work with no prototype equivalent.

Two of them carry gate metrics directly: reminder scheduling decides call completion, and the funnel instrumentation is what makes any of the four measures readable at all.

Two Phase 2 items have a Phase 1 hook

Multi-currency and the referral programme are not built in Phase 1, but each needs one small thing recorded now: a currency on every price, and a referral code captured at signup. Both are listed as features X.10 and K1.07. Neither does anything in the pilot. Both are genuinely expensive to retrofit, because one touches every price in the system and the other requires reconstructing attribution that was never recorded.

Two things stand out. Only 15 percent of the work is hardening, which is the assumption the commercial case rests on, and the cross-cutting bar is the one with no page attached, so it is the one a page-by-page estimate misses entirely. Eleven of the nineteen large features sit there.

The number this document was written to surface

At the indicative bands, 64 small, 84 medium and 19 large features total somewhere between 570 and 1,170 hours, against a Phase 1 budget of roughly 156 hours.

That gap is far too large to be a rounding difference, and it means one of three things is true: the bands are too generous for a small senior team reusing an existing codebase, the feature list is finer-grained than the original estimate assumed, or Phase 1 as inventoried here is genuinely larger than what was priced. It is most likely all three, in that order.

What the document can say with confidence is narrower and more useful: the research report's bottom-up reading of "north of 200 hours" is supported, and the Week 3 scope fix is the right moment to decide what gets cut. This inventory is the list to cut from, because every item has an ID, a size and a stated purpose.

Where the hardening assumption carries the most weight

Only 25 of 167 features are classified as hardening existing work: 10 on customer pages, 12 on creator pages, and 3 cross-cutting. The administrator surface has none at all, because the prototype has no admin tooling. If the prototype turns out to be unreachable, those 25 items become new work and the eleven large cross-cutting items lose their only existing foundation.

The commercial case rests on those 25 being real. It has not been verified.

What to do with this document

  • Before design: read it as a completeness check. Anything missing is cheaper to add now than in Week 9.
  • At the scope fix: use it as the cut list. Every feature has an ID and a size, so trades can be made precisely rather than vaguely.
  • During build: use the IDs on tickets, so a change request can be traced to the feature it displaces under the trade rule.
  • At the end: it is the test plan. Each feature's description is effectively its acceptance criterion.
