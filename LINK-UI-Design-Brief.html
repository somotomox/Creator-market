01

The product in two minutes

LINK is a marketplace where a creator sells a paid one-to-one video call to the people who already follow them. The creator sets a price and some availability, gets one shareable link, and puts it in their bio. A follower opens that link, picks a time, pays by card, and both join a browser-based call. LINK holds the money until the call happens, then pays the creator and keeps 15 percent.

Three things about this product change how it should be designed, and they are not obvious:

The customer is not shopping

They arrive from a creator they already follow and trust. They have chosen the person before they see the page. The profile is a conversion page for a decision mostly made, not a listing competing for attention. Do not design it like a marketplace tile.

The trust problem is about LINK, not the creator

The visitor believes in the person and has never heard of the platform. Design effort belongs on payment protection, refund clarity and processor branding. It does not belong on persuading them the creator is worth it.

Everything happens on a phone

Traffic arrives from social links, and creator onboarding has a stated target of under five minutes on a phone. Design at phone width first and expand. Not the other way round.

The marketplace is empty at launch, and that is the condition you are designing for

Thirty hand-recruited creators, no reviews on day one, thin availability. Emptiness reads as abandonment, not as newness. Do not design surfaces whose emptiness is visible. Browse and category pages exist but are demoted in the pilot, because a category page advertises exactly how small the marketplace is. Every empty state in this product needs a deliberate answer, not a default one.

02

The three user levels

Each level is a separate surface with a separate job. The prototype currently mixes them, and separating them is one of the main things this design pass must fix.

LevelWhoEntry pointSuccess looks likeDesign priority
1 · Creator
The supply side, and the hard side of this marketplace
 Modest following, roughly 20k to 80k. Wants income and credibility without looking unprofessional. A personal invitation from the founder. Never search. A live profile link taking bookings, reached in under five minutes on a phone. Highest
2 · Customer
The demand side
 An existing follower of one specific creator. Has never heard of LINK. A link in a story, bio or post. On mobile. A paid, completed, reviewed call. Then a second one. Highest
3 · Administrator
Internal operations
 One person. At pilot scale this role replaces trust and safety tooling. An alert, a report, or a routine check. The problem resolved before either party escalates. Plain and fast

Structure: three surfaces, one shell

A shared visual language and shared components, but three distinct navigation contexts. A creator setting up their offer and a visitor deciding whether to pay want almost nothing in common, and a single undifferentiated structure serves neither. The administrator surface can be visually plain; it needs speed, not polish.

03

The eleven rules

Each of these comes from research or from an agreed contractual decision. If a design conflicts with one of these, the design changes, not the rule. Every rule carries its reason, because a rule without a reason gets broken the first time it is inconvenient.

R1Show the all-in total before the card form

The service fee must appear in the total at slot selection, not at checkout. The customer should never see a number go up.

Why: unexpected extra costs are cited by 48 percent of people who abandon a checkout, the single largest documented cause. This is the cheapest conversion win in the product and it is free until this screen is drawn.
R2Guest checkout. No account to buy one call

Collect only what the booking requires: name, email, payment. No password, no profile, no confirmation step.

Why: forced account creation is cited by 26 percent of abandoners. The customer is buying one call from one person, not joining a platform.
R3Availability renders in the viewer's time zone, always

Never the creator's. Never UTC. State the zone visibly so the viewer can confirm it.

Why: a visitor who has to do mental arithmetic to work out whether a slot suits them is a visitor who leaves. It is in the acceptance criteria and should be treated as conversion-critical, not as formatting.
R4Payment protection is visible at the pay button

One line: the payment is held until the call is complete. Plus one line of refund policy. Both adjacent to the button, not in a footer or a terms link.

Why: the customer's primary fear is paying and being ghosted, and it peaks at exactly this moment. Held funds are the strongest trust asset the product has and they are currently invisible.
R5The verified badge makes a precise, dated claim

Not "Verified". Instead: 18.2k on Instagram · verified Oct 2026. Manual verification gets a visibly lighter "linked" treatment so the two are never confused.

Why: "Verified" alone implies identity verification, which LINK does not do. A smaller precise claim is more credible than a larger vague one and is defensible if challenged.
R6Payout setup is deferrable, and the copy must prove it

A creator reaches a live profile without entering bank details. The step must look optional, not skippable-but-probably-required.

Why: bank details are the heaviest step in the creator flow and the one most likely to end the session. Deferring it is already the design intent; a step that looks mandatory will be treated as mandatory.
R7Never show an empty price field

Show a suggested price with its basis, and the net amount the creator receives: You receive $34 of $40.

Why: of the four steps to a live profile, three have an obviously correct answer. Pricing does not; it is a self-valuation under uncertainty and the likeliest abandonment point. A suggestion turns an identity question into accept-or-adjust.
R8Design the empty state before the full one

Every list, dashboard and category in this product will be empty at launch. The empty state is the default condition, not the edge case.

Why: emptiness reads as abandonment. A new creator's dashboard should show the next action, not "no bookings yet". Categories with fewer than a handful of creators should not be exposed at all.
R9Mobile first, literally

Draw the phone layout first. Desktop is the expansion. Minimum touch target 44 by 44 points, including availability grids.

Why: mobile cart abandonment runs 80 percent against 66 percent on desktop, and both sides of this marketplace arrive on a phone by construction.
R10Focus states and error messages are conversion features

Visible focus on every interactive element. Error messages that say what went wrong and how to fix it, placed with the field they belong to.

Why: usually argued on accessibility grounds, but in a checkout they are conversion mechanics. A user who cannot tell which field is active, or why a payment was rejected, abandons.
R11No invented numbers, anywhere

No placeholder statistics, no sample review counts, no "join 10,000 creators". If a number is not real and sourced, it does not appear, including in the design file.

Why: the current prototype contains fabricated figures and it is recorded as both a trust problem and a legal risk on a page that takes card payments. Numbers invented in a mockup have a habit of shipping.
04

Foundations

A token set, not a design system. Phase 1 delivers enough shared decisions to make 21 screens coherent. The full documented system in Figma and code is Phase 2, so do not build one now.

Colour

Derived from the existing coral identity in the prototype, which is being kept. Coral is the brand; it is not the interface. Use it for brand moments and the primary action, not for every surface.

Brand and action

Coral, primary action#FF6B5A
Coral pressed#E04A39
Coral tint, surfaces#FFF0EE

Check the contrast. Coral on white is very likely to fail 4.5:1 for body text. It is a button fill with white text, not a text colour. Measure before committing.

Semantic, separate from brand

Success, paid, verified#15803D
Warning, pending#B45309
Error, refunded, failed#B91C1C
Ink, body text#16202E

Money states need their own colours and must never rely on coral. A pending payout and a primary button must not look the same.

Spacing, radius, elevation

Spacing scale

4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64. Use the scale, not arbitrary values. Vertical rhythm matters more than horizontal in a document-heavy product like this.

Radius

Three values only: 8px for inputs and small controls, 12px for cards and panels, full for pills and avatars. More than three reads as inconsistent.

Elevation

Two levels: a hairline border for separation, and one soft shadow for genuinely floating things such as modals and the sticky booking bar. Not every card is elevated.

05

Screens

Twenty-one screens across three levels, in build order. Priority 1 screens carry a gate metric and should be designed first and reviewed hardest.

Layout intent for the three screens that carry the money

These are layout intent, not designs. They exist to settle three arguments before anyone opens a design file: the total appears on the profile and never changes, checkout has no navigation at all, and the dashboard’s empty state is the share prompt rather than a message saying there is nothing here.

Level 2 · Customer design these first

Counterintuitive, but correct: the customer screens contain the two free conversion wins and the hardest trust problem. They also unblock nothing downstream, so getting them wrong is expensive and getting them right early is cheap.

C1Creator profile and booking panelPriority 1
Job
Establish that LINK is legitimate within seconds, then get to a selected slot. The visitor already believes in the creator.
Must contain
  • Verified badge with a precise dated claim (R5)
  • Only real, sourced values. No invented counts (R11)
  • Offer options with duration and price
  • Availability in the viewer's time zone, leading with the nearest slot (R3)
  • The all-in total once a slot is chosen (R1)
  • Refund policy in one line (R4)
Must not
Look like a directory listing. Compete with other creators. Show review counts that do not exist.
States
Default, no availability, creator unverified, loading, creator suspended
Acceptance
A visitor reaches checkout in under 90 seconds. Availability renders in the viewer's own zone.
C2CheckoutPriority 1
Job
Take payment without surprising anyone. This single screen carries the two largest documented abandonment causes.
Must contain
  • Guest checkout. Name, email, card. Nothing else (R2)
  • The same total shown at slot selection, unchanged (R1)
  • Fee line labelled with what it covers: Service fee (card processing)
  • "Your payment is held until the call is complete" at the button (R4)
  • Refund policy line with a link to detail
  • Visible processor branding
Must not
Add any number the customer has not already seen. Require a password. Span more than one screen on mobile.
States
Default, validating, card declined, slot taken while paying, success, network failure
Acceptance
Fully keyboard navigable with visible focus. Errors say what to do next.
C3ConfirmationPriority 2
Job
Reassure immediately, and make the join link impossible to lose.
Must contain
Creator, date and time in the viewer's zone, the join link, what happens next, how to cancel, and the refund window.
States
Default, add-to-calendar taken, link copied
C4Call joinPriority 2
Job
Get both people into the call with no download and no account.
Note
The call interface itself is the video provider's prebuilt UI and is not being designed. What you design is the wrapper: the pre-join screen, the too-early state, the creator-not-here state, and the call-ended state.
States
Too early, ready to join, waiting for the other party, in call, ended, permission denied
Watch
There is no device check in Phase 1. The permission-denied state is the only recovery path and needs real design attention.
C5Post-call reviewPriority 2
Job
Capture a rating, and prompt the second booking.
Must contain
One to five stars, optional text, and a book again affordance beside it. The rebooking prompt is a recommended addition because the primary gate metric is defined as trending up and nothing else in scope encourages recurrence.
States
Prompt, submitted, skipped, call did not happen
C6–C8Landing page, browse, policy pagesPriority 3
Landing
One page, with a clear split between the creator story and the customer story. Replaces the two competing landing pages in the prototype.
Browse
Exists, deliberately demoted. Not the pilot's route to a booking. Design the sparse state as the normal state (R8).
Policy
Refunds, conduct, terms, privacy. Plain, readable, linked from checkout. Content is the client's legal responsibility; the layout is ours.

Level 1 · Creator design second

K1Sign-up and account connectionPriority 1
Job
Get from an invitation link to a connected account with as little friction as possible.
Must contain
A clear statement of what connecting the account does and does not do. Permission anxiety peaks here, before any value has been shown.
Critical branch
If the connection fails, or the creator is on a platform Phase 1 does not automate, the flow must continue with manual verification pending. Never a dead end, never an error message that implies rejection.
States
Default, connecting, connected, connection failed, unsupported platform, already registered
K2Onboarding, three stepsPriority 1
Job
Reach bookable in under five minutes on a phone, unaided. This screen owns a gate metric.
Step 1, offer
Duration from bounded options. Price with a suggestion and its basis, plus the net received (R7).
Step 2, availability
Flat slots in the creator's own zone. Same-day and next-hour presets so first availability is one tap.
Step 3, payouts
Visibly optional (R6). "Add your bank details later. You will need them before your first payout, not before going live."
Must contain
Progress that shows how little is left. A live preview of how the profile will look when shared, because that is what the creator is actually anxious about.
States
Each step: empty, filled, error, saving. Plus an exit-and-resume state.
Acceptance
Bookable without entering bank details. Under five minutes on a phone with no instructions.
K3Dashboard, earnings firstPriority 1
Job
Answer "am I making money and what is next" in one glance.
Must contain
Pending earnings, paid earnings, the next upcoming call, and a prompt for anything still missing from the profile.
Empty state
The most important empty state in the product. A new creator sees this immediately after onboarding, before any booking exists. It must show the next action, such as sharing the link, not "no earnings yet" (R8).
States
Brand new, awaiting first booking, active, payout pending, payout failed, suspended
K4–K7Offer editor, availability editor, bookings list, messagesPriority 2
Offer and availability
The same components as onboarding, in an editing context. Do not design them twice.
Bookings
Upcoming and past, with the join link prominent on anything upcoming.
Messages
Gated: a thread exists only after a confirmed booking. The locked state needs a clear explanation, because this is a deliberate restriction that will be questioned.

Level 3 · Administrator design last, keep it plain

A1–A3Members list, payments list, record detailPriority 2
Job
Locate one record and act on it in two clicks. Speed, not polish.
The five actions
Verify a creator manually, find a member or payment, issue a refund against policy, suspend an account, and respond to a monitoring alert. Each reachable in two clicks from the list.
Must contain
Search, money-state pills, and confirmation on every destructive or financial action. There is no audit log in Phase 1, so every irreversible action needs a confirm step with the consequence stated.
Design budget
Deliberately low. Use the component library as-is. No bespoke layouts.

Every screen needs these states

The most common reason a design hands off incomplete is a missing state. Run this list on every screen before you call it done.

StateQuestion it answersCommonly missed on
DefaultWhat does this look like when everything is normal?—
EmptyWhat does a new creator or an unbooked day look like?Dashboard, bookings, browse
LoadingWhat happens in the seconds after a tap, especially on payment?Checkout, slot picker
ErrorWhat went wrong, and what does the person do now?Checkout, account connection
SuccessHow does the person know it worked?Onboarding steps, refunds
DisabledWhy can this not be used yet, and what would enable it?Pay button, messages
FocusWhich element has keyboard focus? (R10)Everything interactive
Long contentWhat happens with a 40-character name or a 500-word review?Profile, reviews, messages
Phone widthDoes it work at 375px without horizontal scroll? (R9)Tables, availability grid

Three states specific to this product

Slot taken while paying. Two customers can select the same slot. The second one needs a graceful recovery, not a generic error.

Creator unverified. A profile can be live with a pending manual verification. It must still look credible, just differently (R5).

Call window states. Too early, joinable, in progress, missed, completed. The missed state is the one people forget, and it is the one that triggers refunds.

Pattern: name the thing in the user's words, state the consequence, and give the next action. Never make a person infer what happens to their money.

08

What you are not designing

Out of scope for Phase 1. If a design implies any of these, it has grown past the agreement.

Deferred to Phase 2

  • A full documented design system in Figma and code
  • Second and third platform connections
  • Search with ranking and relevance
  • Push and text notification surfaces
  • Pre-call device check and lobby
  • Self-serve rescheduling and calendar sync
  • Availability rules, buffers, blackout dates, recurring slots
  • Dispute, fraud and moderation tooling
  • Trust and safety queues, audit log views
  • Multiple currencies

Beyond Phase 2

  • Native mobile apps. This is a progressive web app in both phases
  • Group calls, packages, subscriptions
  • Tips, gifting, creator pro tier
  • Call recording and automatic summaries
  • Referral and ambassador programme screens
  • Advanced creator analytics
  • Real-time "available now" status

One exception worth knowing: same-day and next-hour availability presets are in scope. They deliver most of the immediacy of a live status for a fraction of the cost.

If you think something is missing, say so before you design it

Phase 1 is a fixed fee against a fixed duration, and additions must displace something of equal size or move to Phase 2. A screen that quietly includes an out-of-scope feature creates a build expectation that was never costed. Raise it as a question, not as a mockup.

What engineering needs from you

  • Tokens as values, not as pictures. Hex codes, spacing numbers, type sizes, radii, in a form that can be copied.
  • Every state as a separate frame, named so it is findable. Not variants hidden behind a toggle.
  • Breakpoint behaviour described where it is not obvious, particularly for the availability grid and any table.
  • Copy in the design, final, not lorem. The strings in Section 07 are part of the design.
  • Interaction notes for anything with timing: loading, success confirmation, the call window countdown.

The one-sentence version of this brief

Design for a phone, for an empty marketplace, for someone who trusts the creator and not the platform, and never let a number appear that the person has not already seen. Everything else in this document follows from those four things.
