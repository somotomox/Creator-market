01

Five principles behind this structure

Each of these comes from the research and each one explains a choice that would otherwise look wrong.

1. The customer does not need navigation

They arrive on one creator's profile from a link, book, attend, and leave. They are not browsing a marketplace. Every navigation link on the booking path is an exit, not a convenience. The public header is therefore deliberately minimal, and the checkout has no navigation at all.

2. The creator does need navigation, because they return

A creator comes back to check earnings, add availability and answer messages. That is a repeat-use app and it gets persistent navigation, app-style, with bottom tabs on mobile.

3. Browse is built but demoted

Category pages advertise exactly how small a launching marketplace is. They exist, they are reachable, and they are not promoted in the pilot. Promote them in Phase 2 once density exists.

4. Three surfaces, one shell

Shared components and one visual language, but three distinct navigation contexts. Mixing creator and fan surfaces is one of the four structural problems in the current prototype.

5. The creator profile is the front door, not the home page

Most pilot traffic never sees /. It lands on /c/{handle}. The profile carries the trust burden that a home page normally carries, and it is designed and reviewed accordingly.

Two structural choices worth defending

Creator profiles sit at /c/{handle}, not at /{handle}. A root-level namespace looks cleaner but permanently collides with every future page: a creator called "help" or "browse" breaks the site. The two extra characters buy a namespace that never has to be migrated.

Booking, checkout, call and review are reached by reference, not by login. A guest customer has no account, so these pages are accessed through a long unguessable reference in the URL, delivered by email. That is what makes guest checkout possible, and the reference must be long enough that it cannot be enumerated.

Page details

How each page works, and what the designer and the developer each need to do about it.

C1Creator profile/c/{handle}Priority 1

How it works. A visitor arrives from the creator's own link, on a phone, having never heard of LINK. The page has to prove the platform is legitimate within seconds, then move them to a selected time slot. Everything above the fold is doing trust work: the creator's photo and name, the verified badge with its dated claim, and the offer with a price. Below that sits availability, rendered in the visitor's own time zone and leading with the nearest slot rather than a month view. Choosing a slot reveals the all-in total including the service fee, which is the last number the visitor should ever see change. This page is not a marketplace listing competing for attention; the visitor has already chosen the person, so the page's only job is removing doubt and getting to checkout fast.

For the UI designer
  • Draw at 375px first. The booking panel becomes a sticky bottom bar on mobile and a side column on desktop.
  • The verified badge reads 18.2k on Instagram · verified Oct 2026. Design the lighter linked variant for manually verified creators at the same time, not later.
  • Availability leads with the nearest slot. Show the viewer's time zone as a label they can see, not as an assumption.
  • No browse, no search, no sign-in prompt anywhere on this page. Nav context B only.
  • How LINK works opens a panel over the page, never navigates away.
  • Design the no-availability state as a first-class state. It will happen often in a pilot.
  • No invented review counts, follower numbers or social proof. If it is not real it does not appear, including in the mockup.
For the developer
  • Resolve {handle} case-insensitively, and permanently redirect retired handles rather than returning 404.
  • Compute availability server-side in UTC, send with offsets, render in the browser's resolved time zone. Never trust a stored local time.
  • Exclude slots inside the booking lead time and any already-held slot. A slot held in another session is unavailable.
  • Return the all-in total from the server, including the service fee, so the client never calculates money.
  • Fire profile_viewed on load and slot_selected on choice. These are two of the four funnel events.
  • Suspended or unverified creators: return a valid page with a reduced state, never a server error.
  • Open graph tags matter here. This link is shared into social feeds and the preview card is the first impression.
States
Default, no availability, creator unverified, creator suspended, loading, handle not found
Acceptance
A visitor reaches checkout in under 90 seconds. Availability renders in the viewer's own zone. Only real, sourced values appear.
Events
profile_viewed, slot_selected
C2Checkout/checkout/{ref}Priority 1

How it works. The single highest-value screen in the product, and the one carrying the two largest documented abandonment causes. The customer arrives with a slot held for them and a total they have already seen. They enter a name, an email and a card. No password, no account, no second step. The page shows the fee line labelled with what it covers, a line saying the payment is held until the call completes, and a line of refund policy, all adjacent to the pay button rather than in a footer. On success the slot converts from held to booked and both parties are emailed within sixty seconds. Navigation is suppressed entirely: the logo is not a link and there is no footer, because every exit on a payment page is a lost booking.

For the UI designer
  • One screen on mobile. If it scrolls past two viewport heights at 375px, cut fields.
  • The total must be visually identical to the figure shown on C1. A customer should never see a number move.
  • Fee line reads Service fee (card processing), not "Service fee".
  • At the button: "Your payment is held until the call is complete." and "Free cancellation up to 24 hours before."
  • Design the held-slot countdown. The customer needs to know the slot is reserved and for how long.
  • Every field needs a visible label, a focus state and an error state. Placeholder-as-label is not acceptable here.
  • Design the card-declined and slot-taken states properly. Both are common and both are recoverable if designed.
For the developer
  • Hold the slot when the checkout reference is created, with a server-side expiry. Release it on expiry or abandonment.
  • Recompute the total server-side at payment time and reject any client-supplied amount.
  • Use Stripe's hosted card element so card data never touches our origin.
  • Capture, then hold. Funds are held until the call completes and the review window passes. Do not release on payment.
  • Idempotency key on the payment call. A double-tapped pay button must not charge twice.
  • Handle the race where the slot is taken during payment: refund immediately and offer the nearest alternative.
  • Fire checkout_started on load and payment_succeeded on confirmation. Completing the four-event funnel.
  • Store the booking-source attribution captured from the referring link, even though nothing uses it in Phase 1.
States
Default, validating, card declined, slot taken while paying, hold expired, success, network failure
Acceptance
Guest only. Fully keyboard navigable with visible focus. The total never changes. Errors say what to do next.
Events
checkout_started, payment_succeeded, payment_failed
C3Confirmation and manage booking/booking/{ref}Priority 2

How it works. Two jobs in one page. Immediately after payment it reassures and hands over the join link. Afterwards it is the page every reminder email links to, and the only place a guest customer can cancel. Because the customer has no account, access is by the long reference in the URL, which is the credential. It shows the creator, the time in the viewer's zone, the join link, what happens next, and the cancellation option with its refund consequence stated plainly rather than linked.

For the UI designer
  • The join link is the most important element on the page at every stage. Treat it as the primary action, not as detail.
  • Show the cancellation consequence as text, not as a policy link: "Cancel now for a full refund" changes to "Cancel now for a 50 percent refund" as the window closes.
  • Add-to-calendar is worth the space. It is the single best defence against a no-show.
  • Design the post-call state. This page does not disappear after the call; it becomes the receipt.
For the developer
  • The reference must be long and random enough that it cannot be enumerated. It is the only access control.
  • Compute the refund tier live from the current time against the call start, not from a stored value.
  • Generate a proper calendar file with the join link inside the event body.
  • Never expose the creator's email or the other party's details on this page.
  • Cancellation triggers the refund path immediately and emails both parties.
States
Just booked, upcoming, within the hour, in progress, completed, cancelled, refunded, creator suspended
Acceptance
Reachable without an account. Refund tier shown is always correct for the current time.
C4Call join/call/{ref}Priority 2

How it works. A thin wrapper around the video provider's prebuilt call interface. We do not design or build the call UI itself. What we own is everything around it: the too-early state, the ready-to-join state, the waiting-for-the-other-party state, the ended state, and the one that matters most, permission denied. There is no pre-call device check in Phase 1, so if a browser refuses camera or microphone access, this page is the only recovery path that exists.

For the UI designer
  • Give the permission-denied state real attention. Browser-specific instructions, because the fix differs on iOS Safari and Android Chrome.
  • The too-early state should show a countdown, not a locked door. The customer is anxious and the countdown is reassurance.
  • Waiting-for-other-party needs a grace timer and a clear statement of what happens if they do not arrive.
  • Minimal chrome. Once the call starts, the provider's UI owns the screen.
For the developer
  • Issue a short-lived room token per participant. Never a shared permanent room URL.
  • Open the room only inside the join window: a few minutes before start until a defined cutoff after.
  • Capture call_joined per party from the provider webhook, not from the client. The completion gate metric depends on it.
  • Start the no-show grace timer server-side when the first party joins.
  • Detect permission failures and route to the recovery state rather than letting the provider show a raw error.
States
Too early, ready, waiting for other party, in call, ended, permission denied, expired, cancelled
Acceptance
Both parties join from a browser on desktop and mobile, no download, no account for the customer.
Events
call_joined per party, call_completed
C5Post-call review/review/{ref}Priority 2

How it works. Reached from the review request email and from the ended state of the call page. One to five stars, optional written text, and beside it the book again affordance. That second element is a recommended addition rather than original scope, and it matters because the primary gate metric is defined as trending up and nothing else in Phase 1 encourages a repeat booking. The review feeds the average rating gate metric and appears on the creator's profile.

For the UI designer
  • Stars first, text optional and visibly optional. A required text field halves submission.
  • Book again sits beside the submit action, not after it. The intent is highest in the moments right after a good call.
  • Design the call-did-not-happen variant. It routes to refund and support, not to a rating.
  • One screen, no scrolling at 375px.
For the developer
  • Only reachable after a completed call, once per booking.
  • Submitting the review ends the review window and releases the payout.
  • Fire review_submitted with the star value. Fire rebook_clicked separately.
  • Book again pre-selects the same creator and offer on C1.
States
Prompt, submitted, already reviewed, call did not happen, expired
Acceptance
Rating and text appear on the creator profile. Payout releases on submission or window expiry.

C6 · Home Priority 3   /

How it works. One page explaining the product to two audiences, replacing the two competing landing pages in the prototype. Split clearly: the creator story with Start earning, the customer story with Browse creators. Most pilot traffic never sees this page.
Designer: do not let the two stories compete for the same space. A visitor should know within one screen which half is for them. Nav context A, full header.

C8 · How LINK works   C9 · Help Priority 2   /how-it-works /help

How they work. C8 is the trust explainer: what held funds mean, what the refund policy is, what verification does and does not claim. It renders both as a standalone page and as a panel opened from the creator profile, so a doubting visitor never navigates away mid-booking. C9 is the support route, which the research flagged as an unscoped gap: a customer whose creator did not show currently has no path except the creator who did not show.
Designer: write C8 as answers to the three questions a nervous customer actually asks. C9 can be a single form or a published monitored address, but it must exist and be findable from the footer and from every booking.
Developer: C8 panel and page share one content source. C9 submissions must reach a monitored inbox and include the booking reference automatically when opened from a booking.

How they work. Refunds, terms, privacy and conduct are plain readable documents linked from the footer and from checkout. Content is the client's legal responsibility; the layout is ours. The 404 matters more than usual here, because a retired or suspended creator handle is a realistic case in a pilot.
Designer: policy pages get real typographic care. People read the refund policy when they are already unhappy. The 404 offers browse and support, never a dead end.
Developer: policy content should be editable without a deploy. Return a real 404 status rather than a 200 with an error page, and log handle-not-found separately so retired links can be spotted.

Onboarding navigation is deliberately stripped

The three onboarding pages carry a progress indicator and a back control, and nothing else. No tabs, no dashboard link, no account menu. A creator who wanders out of onboarding at step 2 does not come back, and creator activation above 55 percent is a gate metric.

Page details

The three onboarding pages carry a gate metric between them. They get the most attention here for that reason.

K1Sign up and connect account/signupPriority 1

How it works. A hand-recruited creator arrives from a founder's message. They create a minimal account, then connect a social account so LINK can import their profile and follower count and set the verified marker. This is where permission anxiety peaks, before any value has been demonstrated, so the page must say plainly what connecting does and does not do. The critical rule is that this step can never dead-end. If the connection fails, or the creator leads on a platform Phase 1 does not automate, the flow continues with a manual verification pending and a lighter linked badge. An administrator confirms later. A creator must never be told their platform is unsupported.

For the UI designer
  • State the permission scope in the creator's words before the OAuth button: what we read, what we never post, what we never see.
  • The failure path needs to look like an alternative route, not an error. Same visual weight as the success path.
  • Minimal account fields. Email and password, or social identity. Nothing optional at this stage.
  • Show what happens next so the creator knows the setup is short. Three steps, roughly five minutes.
For the developer
  • Request the narrowest OAuth scope that returns profile and follower count. Nothing more.
  • Store the follower count with the date it was read. The badge claim is dated and must stay accurate.
  • Handle revoked access later: the badge degrades to linked rather than disappearing.
  • Create the account before the OAuth redirect so a failed callback does not lose the signup.
  • Capture a referral code if present in the URL, even though nothing consumes it in Phase 1.
  • Fire signup_completed. This is the denominator of the activation gate metric.
States
Default, connecting, connected, connection failed, unsupported platform, already registered, email taken
Acceptance
A creator whose connection fails still reaches onboarding. No dead ends.
Events
signup_completed, social_connected, social_failed
K3Onboarding 1 · the offer/onboarding/offerPriority 1

How it works. The creator picks a call duration from bounded options and sets a price. This is the likeliest abandonment point in the entire product, and not for a technical reason: of the four steps to a live profile, three have an obviously correct answer and this one does not. Setting a price on your own time is a self-valuation, and an empty field invites deliberation at exactly the moment deliberation causes people to leave. The page therefore never shows an empty price field. It shows a suggested price with its basis, and the net amount the creator will actually receive, converting an identity question into a simple accept-or-adjust.

For the UI designer
  • Never an empty price input. Pre-fill the suggestion and state where it came from in one short line.
  • Show the net figure live: You receive $34 of $40. Not "85 percent commission", which is abstract.
  • Durations as bounded choices, not a free field. Fifteen, thirty and sixty minutes pending final confirmation.
  • Progress indicator shows how little is left, not how much has been done.
  • No navigation on this page. No tabs, no dashboard link, no account menu. A back control and progress only.
For the developer
  • Suggested price derives from the category and follower band. Keep the rule server-side and configurable, because it will be tuned during the pilot.
  • Validate against the agreed price band. The floor exists because fixed card fees consume very small transactions.
  • Calculate the net figure server-side from the live commission and service fee settings, never hardcoded in the client.
  • Save on every step so a creator can leave and resume without losing work.
  • Fire onboarding_step_completed with the step number. Step-level drop-off is how the activation problem gets diagnosed.
States
Empty with suggestion, edited, below floor, above ceiling, saving, saved, resumed
Acceptance
No empty price field is ever shown. The net amount is visible before the creator commits.
K4Onboarding 2 · availability/onboarding/availabilityPriority 1

How it works. The creator adds times they are free, entered in their own time zone and rendered to every visitor in theirs. Phase 1 supports flat slots only: no recurring rules, no buffers, no blackout dates, all of which are Phase 2. The hard part is not the logic but the interface, because an availability grid is the most difficult component to make usable on a small screen. Same-day and next-hour presets exist so that adding a first slot is one tap rather than a calendar negotiation.

For the UI designer
  • This is the hardest mobile component in the product. Design it at 375px before anything else and keep touch targets at 44 points.
  • Presets first, calendar second. "Today afternoon" and "Next 2 hours" remove the hardest decision.
  • Show the creator's own time zone explicitly, and state that visitors see their own.
  • Thin availability is the normal state in a pilot. Design for three slots looking intentional, not sparse.
For the developer
  • Store every slot in UTC with the creator's zone recorded alongside. Render per viewer.
  • Handle daylight saving transitions explicitly. A slot created before a change must not move after it.
  • Enforce a minimum booking lead time so a slot cannot be booked seconds before it starts.
  • Prevent overlapping slots at save time, not at booking time.
  • Reaching a saved offer plus at least one future slot plus a live profile sets the bookable flag on the creator record. That flag is what the activation metric counts, and it is a field, not a report.
States
Empty, presets shown, slots added, overlapping, past time selected, saving, saved
Acceptance
A first slot can be added in one tap. Slots render correctly across a daylight saving boundary.
K5Onboarding 3 · payouts/onboarding/payoutsPriority 1

How it works. Stripe Connect identity and bank details. This is the heaviest step in the creator flow and the one most likely to end the session, which is why it is deliberately deferrable: the creator reaches a live, bookable profile without entering bank details, and only needs them before the first payout. The design intent already exists; the risk is entirely in the copy. A step that looks mandatory will be treated as mandatory, and a creator who stops here never becomes activated.

For the UI designer
  • The skip control must read as a legitimate route, not as abandoning setup. Use the full sentence: "Add your bank details later. You will need them before your first payout, not before going live."
  • Give the deferral option equal visual weight to continuing. Do not grey it out or shrink it.
  • Design the deferred state on the dashboard at the same time, since that is where the prompt reappears.
  • Explain why a bank connection needs identity details. Unexplained identity requests read as suspicious.
For the developer
  • Use Stripe's hosted onboarding. Do not collect bank or identity data on our own forms.
  • Deferral must not block the bookable flag. A creator can take bookings with payouts pending.
  • Listen for the Connect account webhook and update payout readiness asynchronously. Verification can take days.
  • Block only the payout itself when details are missing, never the booking, and surface that clearly on the dashboard.
  • Fire payout_deferred and payout_connected separately. The ratio is a real signal about this step.
States
Not started, deferred, in progress, pending verification, verified, rejected, restricted
Acceptance
A creator becomes bookable without entering bank details.
K6Dashboard/dashboardPriority 1

How it works. The creator's home, answering one question at a glance: am I making money and what is next. Pending earnings, paid earnings, the next upcoming call, and a prompt for anything still missing from the profile, most often payouts. Its empty state is the most important empty state in the product, because a brand new creator sees it immediately after onboarding, before a single booking exists. "No bookings yet" is a failure message. The right content is the next action: share your link, with the link and a copy button right there.

For the UI designer
  • Design the empty state first and the populated state second. Empty is the condition most creators will be in for their first days.
  • Earnings before everything. Pending and paid as distinct figures, because the difference is the question creators actually have.
  • View my profile prominent. Creators constantly want to see what their audience sees.
  • Missing-payout prompt is persistent but not alarming. It is a reminder, not an error.
  • Nav context D. Four bottom tabs on mobile.
For the developer
  • Pending means held and not yet released. Paid means settled to the bank. Never conflate them.
  • Compute from the ledger, not from a cached balance. Money figures must reconcile with Stripe.
  • The next-call card needs the join link live in the window, and hidden outside it.
  • Derive the completion prompts from actual record state rather than a stored checklist that can drift.
States
Brand new, awaiting first booking, active, payout pending, payout failed, verification pending, suspended
Acceptance
Pending, paid, next call and any missing step are all visible on sign-in without scrolling at 375px.

K0 · Creator landing   K2 · Sign in Priority 2   /join /login

How they work. K0 is the page a hand-recruited creator is sent to before they commit. It explains the offer, the 85 percent, and how little setup is involved. Separate from the home page because the audience and the ask are different. K2 is sign-in and reset for returning creators; customers never sign in during Phase 1.
Designer: K0 leads with earnings and setup time, the two things a creator weighs. One primary action, Start earning. K2 is conventional and should not be reinvented.
Developer: K0 carries the referral or campaign parameter through to signup. K2 needs rate limiting and must not reveal whether an email exists.

K7 · Bookings   K8 · Booking detail Priority 2   /dashboard/bookings

How they work. Upcoming and past bookings, with the join link prominent on anything upcoming. The detail view shows customer, time, amount the creator receives, the join link, and the cancel path with its refund consequence stated plainly.
Designer: upcoming and past are different enough to be separate groups, not a filter. The join link is the primary action on every upcoming row. Cancelling must show what the customer receives before confirming.
Developer: a creator cancellation always refunds in full including the service fee, regardless of timing. Notify the customer immediately. Never expose the customer's full contact details beyond what the call requires.

K9 · Messages Priority 2   /dashboard/messages

How it works. A thread exists only between a customer and a creator with a confirmed booking. It is unavailable before payment, which is a deliberate restriction: ungated messaging is how bookings leak off the platform.
Designer: the locked state needs a clear explanation, because this restriction will be questioned by creators. "Messaging opens once a booking is confirmed."
Developer: enforce the gate server-side, not by hiding the interface. Threads are scoped to a booking. Consider whether a thread closes after the review window, and make that a configurable rule rather than a hardcoded one.

K10 · Profile editor   K11 · Offers   K12 · Availability   K13 · Payouts Priority 2

How they work. The editing counterparts to onboarding. K11, K12 and K13 reuse the onboarding components in an editing context rather than being designed twice. K10 covers bio, photo, handle and verification status, with a live preview of the public profile, which is what creators are genuinely anxious about.
Designer: do not draw the offer and availability editors a second time. Design the editing chrome around the existing components. The profile preview should be the dominant element on K10.
Developer: a handle change must permanently redirect the old URL, because creator links live in bios and old posts. Changes to price or availability must not alter bookings already paid for. K13 reflects live Stripe Connect state rather than a cached copy.

K14 · Settings Priority 3   /dashboard/settings

How it works. Email, password, notification preferences and account deletion. Conventional, and should look it.
Designer: use existing components throughout. Deletion needs a confirmation that states the consequences for upcoming bookings.
Developer: deletion with future bookings must be blocked or must cancel and refund them first. Phase 2 adds channel preferences here when push and SMS arrive, so group notification settings in a way that can grow.

Page details

Five pages holding five actions. The design budget here is deliberately low: this surface needs to be fast and correct, not attractive. Every hour spent making admin beautiful is an hour not spent on creator onboarding, which carries a gate metric.

A1Admin home/adminPriority 2

How it works. The only proactive surface in a role that is otherwise entirely reactive. It shows monitoring alerts, today's calls, and anything needing attention: payouts that failed, refunds that did not reach a customer, creators awaiting manual verification. There is no queue system in Phase 1, so this page is the substitute for one, and the administrator triages by reading it rather than by being routed.

For the UI designer
  • A list of things needing attention, ordered by urgency. Not a dashboard of charts.
  • Each row links directly to the record that fixes it. Two clicks from here to resolved.
  • The empty state is the goal state. "Nothing needs attention" should look like success, not like a broken page.
  • Use the existing component library as-is. No bespoke layouts.
For the developer
  • Derive attention items from real state: failed payouts, failed refunds, pending verifications, calls where only one party joined.
  • Surface uptime and error-tracking alerts here as well as to email, so there is one place to look.
  • Admin access is role-gated server-side on every route, not by hiding navigation.
  • Count administrator actions per week. The research proposes this as an operational load proxy, since the manual model has an unknown breaking point.
States
Clear, items pending, system alert active
Acceptance
Every attention item reaches its resolving record in one click.
A2 & A3Members list and member detail/admin/membersPriority 2

How it works. A searchable list of creators and customers, and a detail view holding two of the five administrator actions: verify a creator manually and suspend an account. Manual verification is what makes a single automated platform connection acceptable for a whole pilot, so it is not a fallback feature, it is load-bearing. Suspension is immediate and has no reporting queue or moderation workflow behind it. Because Phase 1 has no audit log, the confirmation dialog on each irreversible action is effectively the only record that the decision was deliberate.

For the UI designer
  • Search is the primary element on the list, not a filter tucked in a corner. Finding one record is the whole job.
  • Verification needs the evidence next to the action: the claimed handle, the follower count, the date read, and a link out to the actual profile.
  • Suspension needs a confirm step stating the consequences: existing bookings, refunds, and whether the profile goes dark.
  • Show the three verification levels distinctly: verified, linked, pending.
For the developer
  • Search across handle, display name and email, with partial matching. An administrator rarely has an exact value.
  • Manual verification sets the badge to linked with the administrator and timestamp recorded on the record, since there is no audit table.
  • Suspension must define what happens to future bookings. Recommended: cancel and refund in full, notify both parties.
  • A suspended creator's profile returns a valid reduced page, never a server error.
  • Every state-changing admin action needs a confirmation step and a written record on the affected record.
States
List: results, no results, loading. Detail: verified, linked, pending, suspended, deleted
Acceptance
Any member located without a database query. Verify and suspend each reachable in two clicks.
A4 & A5Payments list and payment detail/admin/paymentsPriority 2

How it works. Every payment with its current money state, and a detail view holding the third administrator action: issue a refund against the agreed policy. The detail view shows the booking, the call outcome, and which refund tier applies given the timing, so the administrator applies a rule rather than making a judgement. That distinction matters: the research identified arbitrating without an agreed rule as the weak point of the Phase 1 manual model.

For the UI designer
  • Money states as pills with semantic colour: held, released, refunded, failed, disputed. Never coral, which is the brand colour.
  • The refund action should show the applicable tier and the resulting amount before confirmation, computed rather than typed.
  • Show the call outcome beside the payment. Whether both parties joined is the fact that decides most refunds.
  • Numbers right-aligned with tabular figures so columns scan.
For the developer
  • Compute the refund tier from the cancellation policy and the call outcome. Offer an override, but record that it was an override.
  • The platform fee is returned on creator-side failures and retained on customer no-show. Make that automatic, not a manual decision.
  • Refunds are idempotent and must reconcile against Stripe. Never mark refunded locally before the provider confirms.
  • Search by reference, creator, customer email and amount.
  • Surface a failed refund on A1 as an attention item. A refund that silently fails is the worst outcome in this product.
States
Held, released, refunded in full, refunded in part, failed, disputed, retained on no-show
Acceptance
A refund can be issued and reaches the customer. The applied tier matches the agreed policy.

Two gaps the administrator pages cannot close on their own

There is no audit log in Phase 1. Confirmation dialogs and a timestamp on the affected record are the substitute. That is adequate at thirty creators and inadequate above a few hundred, which is why the audit log is an early Phase 2 item.

The no-show grace period and outcomes were still unspecified when this document was written. Until they are, the refund tier on A5 cannot be computed automatically and the administrator is arbitrating case by case. This is a decision, not a build task, and it sits on the critical path for the payment state machine.

Two sending domains, not one

These eight go from the transactional sending domain. Any marketing send, including the large contact list, goes from a separate marketing domain or provider.

A large cold send damages the sending domain's reputation, and if reminders start landing in spam, call completion falls. Call completion above 80 percent is a Phase 2 gate criterion, so a deliverability mistake would fail the pilot for a reason that has nothing to do with whether the marketplace works. This costs nothing to get right and cannot be undone afterwards.

URL conventions

RuleConventionReason
Creator namespace/c/{handle}Keeps the root namespace free. A creator called "help" or "browse" would otherwise break the site permanently.
Guest access/booking/{ref} with a long random referenceNo account means no login, so the reference is the credential. Long enough that it cannot be enumerated.
Lower case, hyphens/how-it-works, never /howItWorksCase-sensitivity bugs and shareability.
No trailing slashCanonical without, redirect the otherDuplicate URLs split analytics, and this product measures a four-event funnel.
Nested by ownership/dashboard/bookings/{id}The path states who the record belongs to, which makes access rules obvious in code and in analytics.
Stable handlesChanging a handle keeps the old URL redirectingCreators share links that live in bios and old posts. A broken creator link is a lost booking and an unrecoverable impression.

Handle changes are a real risk in a pilot

Creators will rename themselves during the pilot, and the link is already in their bio and in posts. Old handles must redirect permanently rather than 404. This is cheap to build now and effectively impossible to repair once links are circulating.

09

Navigation rules

Nine rules the design should hold to. Each one has a reason, because a rule without a reason gets broken the first time it is inconvenient.

Do

  • Suppress navigation during checkout. Logo not clickable, no footer, no exits.
  • Keep the creator profile header to a logo and one trust link. The visitor has already chosen.
  • Give the creator app four bottom tabs on mobile. Four is the maximum before labels truncate at 375px.
  • Put the refund line beside the pay button, lifted out of the footer, where it will actually be read.
  • Make "View my profile" prominent in the creator app. Creators constantly want to see what their audience sees.

Do not

  • Do not offer browse or search on a creator profile. Alternatives at that moment lose the booking.
  • Do not promote category pages in the pilot. They advertise how small the marketplace is.
  • Do not put navigation on the onboarding steps. A creator who wanders out at step 2 does not come back.
  • Do not build one global nav with items hidden by role. Three contexts, built as three contexts.
