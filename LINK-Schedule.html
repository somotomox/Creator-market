05

Why thirteen weeks, and what moves these dates

What changed from the six-week plan

The original plan assumed all twelve scope decisions closed in Week 1 and every approval returning inside one working day. At day nine, five decisions are still open, the launch niche is unresolved, and two of the remaining dependencies carry multi-day third-party waits that neither side controls: Stripe business verification and the social platform app reviews.

Rather than hold a date that depends on all of that going perfectly, this schedule carries the float explicitly and spends it on the work that was previously compressed: a proper research phase, a full information architecture, and a design pass across every screen instead of a token set applied in a week.

Late dependencies move the week, they do not compress it

Anything in a Needed line above that arrives more than three working days late moves that week's milestone rather than shortening the work, and we confirm that in writing in the same week. It is not a penalty, it is what keeps a fixed fee against a fixed duration honest for both sides.

Two decisions still sit on the critical path

The launch niche is tabled and blocks creator recruitment and every category-specific form. The Stripe entity carries multi-day verification inside it and gates the payments week. Both are Week 2 items, and both are the client's to answer.

Earlier is possible

Thirteen weeks is the committed date, not a target to fill. If the Week 2 items land this week and approvals return inside a working day through design, the build compresses and we launch ahead of 16 December. The date is in the client's hands more than it is in ours.
