01Purpose

What this document does

It freezes the build list

Every capability in Phase 1 is listed below with a written acceptance test. If it is not in Section 4, it is not being built in the six weeks.

It records the decisions

Week 1 exists to close the open questions the proposal deliberately left open. Section 3 is the list; each one must be answered before Week 2 begins.

It sets the measuring stick

The four numbers that decide whether Phase 2 goes ahead are defined and instrumented here, in Week 1, not discovered later.

Reading the markersAnything shown as to confirm is an open item awaiting a decision or a value from you. Every one of them appears in Section 3 with the options and the consequence of each. When all are closed and both signatures are on Section 12, this document becomes version 1.1 “locked” and Week 2 starts.
02The objective

Phase 1 answers one question

Do real creators onboard, and do real customers book and pay? Every scope decision in this document is settled by that question. A capability is in Phase 1 if the pilot cannot produce a completed, paid call without it, and out of Phase 1 if the pilot can run without it for six weeks in one niche.

The rule we appliedPhase 1 does not cut features, it builds each one to pilot depth: enough to take real money from real customers in one niche, in one country, and nothing more. Phase 2 takes the same list and hardens it for scale. This is why the scope table below reads as narrow versions of familiar capabilities rather than a shorter list.
03Decisions to close this week

Twelve open items

These are the questions the proposal left open by design. Each one changes what gets built, so each must be answered before Week 2. Decisions taken here are written back into Section 4 and this document is reissued as the locked version.

D1Which social platform does Phase 1 connect?

Phase 1 builds one platform connection plus a manual verification fallback. Platform app review takes longer than the pilot itself, so we submit all reviews in Week 1 and ship with whichever integration is most valuable to the launch niche.

Options: Instagram · TikTok · YouTube. Consequence: determines the onboarding import, the verification badge, and which creator cohort onboards most smoothly.
Decision
D2Which niche is the beachhead?

The pilot recruits twenty-five to fifty creators in a single niche. Narrower is better, liquidity in one category beats thin coverage across many.

Proposal recommendation: fitness and wellness coaches. Consequence: sets the recruiting list, the category copy, and the seed content.
Decision
D3The Phase 1 fee, within the agreed range

The proposal fixes Phase 1 at $8,000 to $10,000, with the exact figure set at this milestone once scope depth is confirmed.

Consequence: the low end assumes the leanest reading of every row in Section 4; the high end assumes the fuller reading of the design and admin rows.
Agreed fee
D4Call durations and the price band creators may set

Creators need bounded options rather than a free-text price field, both for trust and to keep the checkout simple.

Suggested: 15, 30 and 60 minutes; price band from a floor to a ceiling per call. Consequence: affects the offer editor, the profile, and payment minimums.
Durations / band
D5Refund, no-show and cancellation policy

Funds are held until the call completes. We need the exact rules before checkout is built, because they drive the payment state machine.

To specify: customer cancellation window · creator cancellation handling · no-show grace period · who is refunded and how fast · what happens to the platform fee on a refund.
Policy
D6The customer service fee

The proposal recommends charging card-processing costs to the customer as a small service fee, so creators genuinely net the full 85 percent of their sticker price.

To specify: the percentage and any fixed component, and how it is labelled at checkout. Consequence: if declined, creators net roughly 82 percent rather than 85, and the “you keep 85%” promise must be reworded everywhere.
Decision
D7Video provider

Phase 1 uses a managed provider with its prebuilt call interface. Both candidates are equivalent for pilot purposes; the choice is commercial.

Options: Daily · LiveKit. Consequence: per-minute cost and which account you open in Section 9.
Decision
D8Product name and domain

“LINK” is the working prototype name. If it is changing, it must change before Week 2 design begins, not after.

To specify: final product name, primary domain, and whether a rename is in Phase 1 scope at all.
Name / domain
D9Creator cohort size and who recruits them

The pilot depends on hand-recruited creators. The proposal assumes twenty-five to fifty, personally guided to their first paid call.

To specify: target number, who owns outreach, and whether a founder-led list already exists. Consequence: this is the single largest risk to the gate metrics.
Cohort / owner
D10Who owns the Stripe and third-party accounts?

Payment and video accounts should be in the client's name from day one so nothing needs migrating later.

To specify: the legal entity for Stripe Connect, and whether third-party costs are billed to your accounts directly or passed through at cost.
Account owner
D11Launch date and the gate review date

Week 6 is go-live. The gate review sits four weeks after launch, so there is real pilot data to read.

To specify: kickoff date, and therefore the Week 6 launch date and the Week 10 gate review date.
Kickoff / launch / gate
D12Named decision-maker and review cadence

A fixed-fee six-week build needs one person who can approve within a working day. Weekly demos are the default cadence.

To specify: the single point of contact, the demo day, and the shared channel.
Contact / cadence

10Assumptions

What this scope takes as given

Product and market

  • United States only, single currency, single language
  • One niche and one social platform at launch
  • Twenty-five to fifty creators, hand-recruited
  • Commission at fifteen percent, creators keep eighty-five

Delivery

  • The existing prototype codebase is available and usable from Week 1
  • Approvals arrive within one working day of each demo
  • Third-party running costs are billed to your accounts, roughly $60 to $250 a month at pilot scale
  • Legal review of terms, privacy and conduct policies is the client's responsibility

If any assumption proves false, the affected rows in Section 4 are re-estimated under Section 11 before work continues on them.

11Change control

How scope changes after this signature

1. Raise it

Any change, from either side, goes on the shared task list as a written request. Nothing is built on a verbal agreement.

2. Price it

We estimate it within one working day, in hours, and state what it displaces. Phase 1 is a fixed fee against a fixed 156 hours.

3. Choose

Three options: trade it against something of equal size in Section 4, defer it to Phase 2, or extend the fee and the timeline in writing.

The trade ruleBecause Phase 1 is fixed in both fee and duration, additions must displace something. A request that adds twelve hours removes twelve hours from elsewhere in Section 4, or it moves to Phase 2. This is what protects the six-week launch date, and it is the only reason a fixed price at this scale is possible.
