Where the product can win and where it can quietly fail. 24 items across four quadrants, then the cross-quadrant strategies, including the defensive pairings where a weakness removes the ability to contain a threat.

The last tile is the honest one. This is a desk study: everything in it comes from documents, published research and the earlier prototype audit, not from talking to creators or customers. Section 28 of the research report sets out exactly what that costs and how to fix it.

If you only read four things

The findings that change what gets built, with where to read more.

FindingWhy it mattersWhere
Start concentrated, stay universal Marketplaces that survive almost all launched narrow. The product can stay universal while the first thirty creator invitations concentrate in one or two categories. Research §21
Two free conversion wins Show the full price before the card screen, and allow booking without an account. These address the two largest documented abandonment causes and cost nothing until the screens are drawn. Research §14
Email-only reminders put a gate metric at risk Call completion above 80 percent decides Phase 2. Text reminders substantially outperform email, so a shortfall could be channel choice rather than product quality. Research §21
167 features against 156 hours A bottom-up inventory comes to several times the budgeted effort. The scope fix is the moment to cut deliberately rather than quietly during build. Features §07

Two things still open

The launch niche is unresolved and blocks creator recruitment, the category taxonomy and the intake forms. Prototype access is unconfirmed, and the commercial case rests on the existing build being reachable and usable. Both are client decisions and both were open when these documents were issued.
